Modules 1 and 2 were done previously; **24 modules were written today.**

Every lesson now carries:

- **`teach.lead`** — the teaching, before any question is asked
- **`teach.terms`** — each term with a plain definition *and* an everyday comparison (`like`)
- **`teach.close`** — the point the lesson is actually making
- **`check`** — two questions, with an explanation shown either way

### House style, held across all 26 modules

- Second person, addressed to one student. Never "the student", never "students".
- **Everyday comparison first, jargon second.** Every single term carries a `like`.
- State the correction plainly instead of hedging it.
- **No repeated risk disclaimers.** The site footer carries the standing one. Module 101 says it
  once — *"this course teaches how the instrument works, it does not tell you what to buy"* — and
  then does not say it again.
- Analogies deliberately recur across modules so the tracks build on each other.

### The teaching each module hangs on

**Forex**

| Module | The idea it turns on |
|---|---|
| 3 Orders, leverage, margin | Leverage is **permission, not borrowed money**. Two identical trades at 30:1 and 500:1 lose exactly the same. The danger is the temptation, not the mechanism. |
| 4 Brokers and setup | The "commission-free" broker at 1.6 pips versus 0.2 + 0.7 = 0.9 pips. Nearly twice as expensive. |
| 5 Sessions | Reframes "my broker hunted my stop": you put your stop where everyone else put theirs, and price goes where the orders are. Removes the paranoia, keeps the insight. |
| 6 Reading price | Trend as a **mechanical sequence** two people can agree on, not the shape of the picture. Ends on the five-sentence narration script. |
| 7 Levels | A level is **inventory, not magic**. Levels get *weaker* with each test — the bakery runs out of loaves. |
| 8 Indicators | Every indicator is arithmetic on the past. ATR sold properly, with the fixed-25-pip stop worked through in both directions. |
| 9 Fundamentals | Exam results: 85% is a disappointment when everyone predicted 90. Closes on hidden correlation — umbrella shop, raincoat shop, wellington shop are one bet on rain. |
| 10 Risk | **Position size is an output, not a choice.** A joiner measures the gap before cutting the wood. A £100 shirt needs a 100% rise to undo a 50% fall. |
| 11 Strategy | A setup is a recipe someone else could cook from. Overfitting = memorising last year's exam paper. |
| 12 Testing & psychology | **Discipline is the wrong tool.** Willpower is lowest when the market is hardest. You don't need more willpower, you need to not buy the biscuits. |

**Equities**

| Module | The idea it turns on |
|---|---|
| 101 What a share is | Indices survive by quietly **replacing their failures** — a school boasting about average grades after the strugglers have been moved on. "It always comes back" is a claim about indices, not your holding. |
| 102 Pricing and trading | First time a student can *see* the mechanism module 1 described. Market impact and halts framed as sizing decisions. |
| 103 Shares vs CFDs | Buying a house versus betting on house prices. Hard rule: **no leverage through a scheduled earnings date.** |
| 104 Reading the numbers | Revenue is vanity, profit is opinion, **cash is fact**. A low P/E is a question; a soaring yield is usually a collapsed price. |
| 105 Earnings and events | Holding through earnings unleveraged is a decision. **Not knowing the date is not.** |
| 106 Indices and sectors | Most of a share's daily move has nothing to do with the company. **Five banks is one position.** Fund overlap catches careful people. |
| 107 Risk without leverage | A 1% risk is a 12.5% position. A gap bypasses the stop entirely, so **concentration matters as much as risk**. The 40% gap test. |
| 108 Building an approach | **Horizon drift** — a losing trade promoted to "a long-term investment" is a stop loss removed with extra steps. |

**Bonds**

| Module | The idea it turns on |
|---|---|
| 201 What a bond is | Third relationship: a bet, then ownership, now lending. Landlord versus business partner. **"Defined", not "safe."** |
| 202 Price and yield | The inverse relationship *derived*, not asserted: your locked 4% savings account when banks start paying 6%. Then an 8% loss with nobody defaulting. |
| 203 Duration | Duration is this track's ATR — the number you size from. A see-saw: distant money swings hardest. |

## 4. Tooling built

### `tools/check-teaching.mjs` (new)

Loads every module and lesson file and reports:

- which modules teach and which still fall back to slides
- instructor-register wording in student-facing text
- terms missing their everyday comparison
- answer indices pointing at a non-existent option
- teaching blocks with no lead, or teaching with nothing to check

Exits non-zero on any problem. **It caught two real defects that would otherwise have shipped** —
an instructor-register objective in module 12 and another in module 9's lesson 4.

```bash
node tools/check-teaching.mjs          # full report
node tools/check-teaching.mjs --todo   # just the unwritten module ids
```

Current output: `26/26 modules teach. All written.`

### `tools/bump-assets.mjs` (existing, run 12 times today)

Asset version went `v=2` → `v=14`. Must run before any deploy touching `assets/` or `content/`,
or students keep the cached old lessons.

## 7. Coverage — what this course does and does not teach

### Taught as full tracks

| Instrument | Coverage |
|---|---|
| **Forex** — currency pairs | 12 modules, 48 lessons, 7 assessed drills |
| **Equities / stocks / shares** | 8 modules, 24 lessons |
| **Bonds / fixed income** | 6 modules, 12 lessons |

### Taught inside those tracks, not as separate tracks

- **CFDs** — module 103 in depth (owning versus CFD, financing, dividends, shorting); module 1
  distinguishes spot from CFD
- **Gold (XAU/USD)** — a tradeable instrument in the simulator, used in examples throughout
- **Indices and ETFs** — module 106 (weighting, tracking, what a fund removes and what it leaves)
- **Corporate credit** — module 204 (spreads, ratings, recovery rates)
- **Short selling and borrow cost** — module 103

### NOT covered

Stating these plainly so nobody assumes otherwise:

- **Options** — no coverage at all. No calls, puts, strikes, premium or greeks.
- **Futures** — one passing mention in module 1 (spot versus CFD versus futures). No track.
- **Cryptocurrency** — one passing mention of decimal conventions in module 2. No track.
- **Commodities beyond gold** — no oil, no agriculture, no metals track.
- **Spread betting** — not covered.

### Platforms

**Taught:** MT4, MT5 and TradingView (module 4 — choosing one, chart templates, server time,
installing indicators); broker types and the equity order book (modules 4 and 102); and the
academy's own simulator terminal, which the drills run on.

**Not taught:** any options or futures platform.

### The real gap worth knowing about

**The 7 assessed drills are all forex.** They attach to forex modules 2, 3, 8, 10, 10, 11 and 12,
and the simulator only carries four instruments — EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY and gold.

So the **equities and bonds tracks teach and test in writing, but have no practical drills.** A
student finishes those tracks having read and answered, not having done. That is the largest
remaining gap in the platform, and closing it means either adding equity/bond instruments to the
engine, or writing drills that assess the analysis rather than the trading — a screening exercise,
a thesis with its invalidation, a duration sizing calculation.
