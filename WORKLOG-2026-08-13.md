Every lesson now carries:

- **`teach.lead`** — the teaching, before any question is asked
- **`teach.terms`** — each term with a plain definition *and* an everyday comparison (`like`)
- **`teach.close`** — the point the lesson is actually making
- **`check`** — two questions, with an explanation shown either way

### House style, held across all 26 modules

- Second person, addressed to one student. Never "the student", never "students".
- **Everyday comparison first, jargon second.** Every single term carries a `like`.
- State the correction plainly instead of hedging it.
- **No repeated risk disclaimers.** The site footer carries the standing one. Module 101 says it
  once — *"this course teaches how the instrument works, it does not tell you what to buy"* — and
  then does not say it again.
- Analogies deliberately recur across modules so the tracks build on each other.

**Forex**

## 8. Addendum — the equities and bonds gap is now closed

Written straight after the above, in the same session.

Section 7 named the largest remaining hole: the seven assessed drills were all forex, so students
finished the equities and bonds tracks having read and answered but never having *done* anything.

**Seven analysis labs now cover those tracks**, bringing the practical total to 14.

They are worked exercises over supplied data rather than simulator sessions. Putting shares and
bonds into the engine would have been the wrong fix — those tracks turn on judgement rather than
execution, and a faked order book or invented earnings gap teaches the wrong thing.

| Lab | Module | What the student actually does |
|---|---|---|
| What the order book actually costs you | 102 | Walks a depth ladder to fill 1,800 shares, finds the £11 of market impact that never appears on a contract note |
| Two companies, six numbers | 104 | Derives EPS, P/E and payout ratio, and identifies the value trap from cash flow and debt rather than the headline ratio |
| Count what you actually own | 106 | Groups holdings by sector, finds banking already at 32.5%, and finds the company held twice via the ETF |
| Size it, then gap it | 107 | Sizes at 1% risk to 625 shares, sees it is a 12% position, then finds a 40% overnight gap cost 4.8% |
| Rates move, and your bond moves | 202 | Watches a bond fall 8.6% with nobody defaulting, then separates opportunity cost from permanent loss |
| Which bond hurts most | 203 | Ranks four bonds by duration and works out what reaching for yield really bought |
| Read the curve, then follow it through | 205 | Identifies the inverted curve, works out which end moved, traces one decision through all three markets |

Three task types: a numeric answer marked to a tolerance, a multiple choice, and sorting items
into buckets. **All answers must be right to pass**, retries are unlimited, and the reasoning shows
either way — a lab that only reports a score teaches nothing.

Labs record into `progress.drills` exactly as terminal drills do, so the path engine, the
certificates and the Trading Floor listing needed no changes beyond routing and not assuming every
drill has an instrument.

**New files:** `content/labs.js`, `assets/labs.js`, `assets/labs.css`. **Changed:** `assets/app.js`
(routing, listing split into simulator drills and analysis labs), `content/tracks.js` (seven new

## 9. Addendum — three new tracks: futures, options and crypto

Written in the same session. The course went from three markets to six.

| Track | Modules | Lessons | Labs | Unlocks after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Futures | 5 (301–305) | 10 | 3 | Bonds certificate |
| Options | 6 (401–406) | 12 | 4 | Futures certificate |
| Crypto & Digital Assets | 5 (501–505) | 10 | 3 | Options certificate |

**Course total: 42 modules, 116 lessons, 24 practical pieces, six stacked certificates.**

The chain order is pedagogical rather than alphabetical. Futures builds directly on forex margin
and equity exchange mechanics. Options is the hardest instrument and assumes everything before it.
Crypto is deliberately last: technically the simplest of the six to start trading, and the one
where custody, leverage and correlation can end you fastest.

### What each track turns on

**Futures.** Corrects the reputation first — this is the oldest risk-transfer tool there is, so the
farmer comes before the speculator, and *"the futures leg is supposed to lose when the business
wins"* is the sentence that makes hedging make sense. Three things genuinely differ from every
earlier track and each gets a lesson: daily cash settlement (an open loss is a withdrawal, so
running out of money and being wrong are two separate ways to lose), whole-contract sizing that
frequently makes the minimum position larger than a correct one, and roll cost — invisible on
statements and, in the lab, **150 times** the commission people actually shop around for.

**Options.** Everything hangs off one asymmetry: the buyer gets a right, the seller takes an
obligation. Insurance carries the framing throughout. The module that decides whether a student
succeeds or bleeds is 403 — *a bought option loses money while nothing happens*, which contradicts
four tracks where a flat market was neutral. The greeks come **last** on purpose: every exposure has
already been felt, so it is labelling rather than memorising.

**Crypto.** Kept deliberately unglamorous, at the user's explicit instruction. Module 502 is the most
valuable in the track and is not about price at all — the list of what has actually cost retail
holders the most ends on the line that matters: *not one of them is being wrong about the price.*
Liquidation cascades explain the market's violence without invoking manipulation, and funding is
framed as the replacement for expiry so the roll-cost lesson transfers intact.

### The structure the course now has

Module 505 — the last lesson of all six tracks — names what a student has been inside without
seeing:

- **One risk policy**, written in module 10 and extended five times. Never replaced.
- **One verdict on analytical tools**, met five times: open interest, credit spreads, positioning,
  implied volatility, the greeks. Context, not signals, every time.
- **One return shape learned to distrust**, met four times: martingale, carry, option selling, high
  leverage. Many small wins, one enormous loss.
- **One correlation lesson**, met three times: dollar-negative pairs, five banks, ten tokens.
- **Three explicit permissions to decline a market on the arithmetic** — futures, options, crypto.
