## 9. Addendum — three new tracks: futures, options and crypto

Written in the same session. The course went from three markets to six.

| Track | Modules | Lessons | Labs | Unlocks after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Futures | 5 (301–305) | 10 | 3 | Bonds certificate |
| Options | 6 (401–406) | 12 | 4 | Futures certificate |
| Crypto & Digital Assets | 5 (501–505) | 10 | 3 | Options certificate |

**Course total: 42 modules, 116 lessons, 24 practical pieces, six stacked certificates.**

The chain order is pedagogical rather than alphabetical. Futures builds directly on forex margin
and equity exchange mechanics. Options is the hardest instrument and assumes everything before it.
Crypto is deliberately last: technically the simplest of the six to start trading, and the one
where custody, leverage and correlation can end you fastest.

### What each track turns on

**Futures.** Corrects the reputation first — this is the oldest risk-transfer tool there is, so the
farmer comes before the speculator, and *"the futures leg is supposed to lose when the business
wins"* is the sentence that makes hedging make sense. Three things genuinely differ from every
earlier track and each gets a lesson: daily cash settlement (an open loss is a withdrawal, so
running out of money and being wrong are two separate ways to lose), whole-contract sizing that
frequently makes the minimum position larger than a correct one, and roll cost — invisible on
statements and, in the lab, **150 times** the commission people actually shop around for.

**Options.** Everything hangs off one asymmetry: the buyer gets a right, the seller takes an
obligation. Insurance carries the framing throughout. The module that decides whether a student
succeeds or bleeds is 403 — *a bought option loses money while nothing happens*, which contradicts
four tracks where a flat market was neutral. The greeks come **last** on purpose: every exposure has
already been felt, so it is labelling rather than memorising.

**Crypto.** Kept deliberately unglamorous, at the user's explicit instruction. Module 502 is the most
valuable in the track and is not about price at all — the list of what has actually cost retail
holders the most ends on the line that matters: *not one of them is being wrong about the price.*
Liquidation cascades explain the market's violence without invoking manipulation, and funding is
framed as the replacement for expiry so the roll-cost lesson transfers intact.

### The structure the course now has

Module 505 — the last lesson of all six tracks — names what a student has been inside without
seeing:

- **One risk policy**, written in module 10 and extended five times. Never replaced.
- **One verdict on analytical tools**, met five times: open interest, credit spreads, positioning,
  implied volatility, the greeks. Context, not signals, every time.
- **One return shape learned to distrust**, met four times: martingale, carry, option selling, high
  leverage. Many small wins, one enormous loss.
- **One correlation lesson**, met three times: dollar-negative pairs, five banks, ten tokens.
- **Three explicit permissions to decline a market on the arithmetic** — futures, options, crypto.

### The simulator now covers shares, futures and options

This was the largest engineering item in the project and it closes three gaps where the course could
describe something but not demonstrate it.

**Engine extension.** Instrument specs gained `kind` (`fx` | `share` | `future`). Only three places

### Lessons now carry code

`assets/lesson.js` gained a `teach.code` block — an optional array of `{ caption, lines, note }`

The code is MQL5 and deliberately short. Nobody learns a language from a course like this; the point
is that a student can read an EA they did not write and find the bug.

### The three labs, and why they are arithmetic rather than syntax

| Lab | The finding |
|---|---|
| `au-guards` | A correct rule with no guards attempts **10,800 orders in 3 bars.** Two guards, six lines, take it to 3 |
| `au-size` | One hard-coded 0.5 lots risks 0.5% on a tight stop and **2% on a wide one** — then **5% after a drawdown**, because the position does not shrink with the account |
| `au-report` | A 94% win rate where **one loss undoes 16.4 average wins**, on a backtest with invented intrabar prices |

None of these is about strategy, and that is the point — it is what actually destroys automated
accounts. `au-size` is the one worth reading twice: the bug is invisible in a backtest that starts
at a fixed balance and only bites in a drawdown, which is the worst possible moment to discover it.

## 12. Addendum — the in-browser EA editor

Written 14 August 2026. The automation track taught EAs and showed code, but a student wrote and
tested theirs in MetaTrader, outside this site. This closes that.

### What it is

A fourth practical surface. A drill with `kind: 'code'` opens an editor; the student's JavaScript
runs in a **Web Worker** against the same `assets/engine.js` the simulator drills use, and is marked
on **what the EA did**, never on what the source says.

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `assets/ea-runtime.js` | The Worker. MQL5-shaped API, mid-bar terminal restart, run report |
| `assets/ea-editor.js` | Editor, console, marking, and the run deadline |
| `content/ea-tasks.js` | Five graded labs for modules 802–806 |
| `tools/check-ea-tasks.mjs` | Proves each is solvable, non-trivial and deterministic |

### The five labs

| Lab | What it makes you meet |
|---|---|
| `ea-guards` (802) | Run the unguarded rule: **3,840 order attempts, 3,775 of them a second order on a bar already traded, 98 positions open at once, 99.5% of the account gone.** Then fix it in six lines |
| `ea-translate` (803) | A plan in English with one vague clause. Three parts become code; *"I get out when it goes against me"* does not — that one was a feeling, and the stop is what it becomes |
| `ea-size` (804) | Replace a hard-coded `0.50` with module 10 as a function. Marked on every trade risking 1%, and on size moving **inversely** to stop distance across the whole run |
| `ea-limits` (805) | An EA that sizes off a stop distance it never attaches, and never stops itself. Add both — the hard part is refusing to un-trip the switch |
| `ea-restart` (806) | The terminal restarts mid-bar. The guard everyone writes acts twice, silently. **No backtest anywhere can produce this** |

### What the checker caught before any of it deployed

Three real defects, each of which would have wasted a student's evening:

1. A volatility gate set at **18 pips** on a feed whose ATR never drops below **32** — the condition
   could never be true, so the "correct" answer traded nothing.
2. A gold stop clamp that pinned **every** trade to the same distance, making the sizing lab's
   central point — size varies with the stop — impossible to demonstrate.
3. The restart landing on a **bar boundary**, where it cannot cause the duplication it exists to
   teach, because the EA has not acted on that bar yet. The replacement bar was found by sweeping
   every candidate for one where the naive guard duplicates and the correct one does not.

### Two engineering findings worth recording

**`Feed.series()` is O(entire history) per call.** Right for `terminal.js`, which calls it once per
rendered frame; ruinous here, where indicators are read several times per tick. One 120-bar run
measured **15 seconds**. Folding in only the new M1 bars took it to **69ms** — a 200× difference,
and the reason the labs are usable at all.

**A plain `setTimeout` deadline accuses correct code of an infinite loop.** Browsers throttle
background tabs, delaying both the timer *and* delivery of the worker's reply. Measured: a five-bar
run took **23.7 seconds** to report back from a hidden tab. The deadline now accumulates only
visible time, and the 120-second wall-clock backstop says the page was backgrounded rather than
blaming the student. This was found by testing rather than reasoned about in advance.

### Verification

- `tools/check-ea-tasks.mjs`: **all 5 labs solvable, non-trivial and deterministic.**
- Driven through the real editor on the live site: the `ea-guards` starter failed 3 of 4 checks with

## 13. Addendum — messaging

Written 14 August 2026, after Jonathan asked whether instructor and student could message each other.
They could not, in either direction. Now they can.

### What was actually there before

Worth recording, because it looked closer to working than it was:

- **`instructorNote`** — a field the Worker accepted, instructor-only, capped at 600 characters. Grep
  across `assets/` and `content/` returned **zero** references. Nothing wrote it and nothing showed
  it. Half a channel, invisible at both ends.
- **The student's weekly demo-log note** — 300 characters, the only free text a student could write.
  `listStudents` returns `modules` and `drills` only, so **the instructor could not see it.**
- **"Ask your instructor"** appeared in three places with no name, no address and no route.
- **The dashboard's "Open student" and "Help" buttons** both navigated to `#/instructor` — the admin
  page, showing nothing about that student. Now fixed: they open that student's conversation.

### What was built

One thread per student, the instructor at the other end of all of them. `student_id` **is** the
thread — there is no recipient column, so student-to-student messaging is not merely disabled, it is
inexpressible in the schema.

Access is decided in one function, `threadIdFor()`, and enforced on the server. Tested against the
live Worker rather than reasoned about:

| Attempt | Result |
|---|---|
| Student reads another student's thread by id | **403** |
| Student posts into another student's thread | **403** |
| Student calls `/api/admin/threads` | **403** |
| No token | **401** |
| Empty body | **400** |

### The XSS test

A student sent `<img src=x onerror=...><script>...</script> **not bold**` and the instructor's view
was checked: **zero img tags, zero script tags, zero bold tags, `window.__XSS` still false**, and
the payload displayed as literal text. Bodies are escaped and only newlines become breaks. No
markdown, deliberately — the moment a student can write markup that renders in the instructor's
browser, it stops being correspondence.

### Verified end to end in the browser

Student sends (line breaks survive) → instructor's list shows it with the unread count and sorts that
conversation to the top → instructor opens it, which clears the unread → instructor replies →
badge goes to hidden. Both sides driven through the real UI, signed in as real sessions.

### Test data

Two throwaway students and three sessions were created for this, then deleted one statement at a
time with `meta.changes` checked on each — because a multi-statement `--command` only runs the
first, which is how a test row survived a cleanup earlier in this project. Final state confirmed:
**two accounts (instructor and demo), zero messages, both progress rows untouched.**

### Naming

Students see **Jonathan Afolayan** throughout, taken from `BRAND.signatory` so the messaging view,
the locked-step link and the certificate cannot drift apart. "Mr. Jonathan" was considered and not
used, at Jonathan's choice.
